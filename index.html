About Me

I approach performance problems as algorithm problems: model the workload as a dependency graph of computation and data movement, set theoretical lower and upper bounds, and find where the system falls short — usually mis-estimated costs, operations serialized without a real dependency, or a structure that blocks better scheduling.

The approach has held across very different systems: encrypted LLM inference (1 hour → 1 minute per block), encrypted vector search (300 ms → 3 ms, shipping as enVector), NPU compiler scheduling (days → minutes), and local TTS (RTF 12.0 → 0.9). It comes from my Ph.D. in algorithms at POSTECH, where I gave an optimal O(n log n)-time algorithm for the Euclidean planar 2-center problem; the same geometric background led me to vector search and ANN indexing.

Separately, working with approximate homomorphic encryption on encrypted LLM inference and encrypted vector search gave me experience in numerical and statistical error analysis.
